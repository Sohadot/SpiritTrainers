Observation

The practitioners exist. The institutions exist. The market exists. The category name does not.

Human development has expanded into hundreds of disciplines, certifications, and brands. Yet no concise, category-level expression has emerged for those whose purpose is the deliberate cultivation of human character, resilience, judgment, and inner strength. It is not the activity that is missing. It is the word.

Implications

Names do not describe categories. They constitute them.

A diffuse practice stays a scatter of instances until a word gathers it into a category. The word becomes the coordinate system: search routes through it, competitors position against it, practitioners describe themselves with it. A category name is less a label than a piece of a market's infrastructure — expensive to be without, and owned by whoever supplies it.

Some names describe companies. Some names describe products. A few become the language through which an entire category is understood. Those names are not manufactured; they are recognized — usually late, usually by whoever was paying attention first.

The name is either an asset on your balance sheet, or a headline on a competitor's.
