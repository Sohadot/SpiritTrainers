Buyer Fit

Who could own this category?

Not a domain, not traffic, not a codebase. Three things a name confers and a URL cannot.

What is actually acquired

  • Default positioncompetitors must name themselves relative to the category owner
  • Optionalitya brand, a division, an institute, a certification — or a defensive hold
  • Denialsecuring the cleanest name means no competitor can have it

The archetypes

Each sees a different value in the same name.

Coaching & Development Platforms

Already in the category, but under a generic or founder-named brand. This is the umbrella name above them.

Fears losing: a competitor consolidating the category first

Corporate Wellbeing & Resilience

Reframes soft "wellbeing" as serious "training" — methodical, outcome-oriented, harder to commoditize.

Fears losing: being reduced to a perk vendor

Leadership Institutes & Universities

An institutional, non-sectarian name for a center on character, judgment, and human development.

Fears losing: relevance to a generation that wants depth without dogma

Military & First-Responder Readiness

"Resilience training" is already the language; this is its clean, unifying name.

Fears losing: nothing — the name dignifies work already funded

Sports & Mental Performance

A category name for "the inner game" that belongs to no single athlete or method.

Fears losing: their edge to a rival that names the space first

EdTech & Youth Development

A warmer, broader frame than the politically loaded "character education."

Fears losing: the framing war over the purpose of education

Three ways of deciding

Strategic

"If I don't own this, who does — and what does that cost me?"

Brand

"Could this become the name people say instead of what I call myself?"

Defensive

"Is this cheaper to own than to compete against?"

Why the name is hard to replace

"Can't we just find another name?"

Short, positive, cross-cultural, two-word category names are structurally scarce. Every strong alternative is already owned, too niche, too clinical, or too clever to become a default. The rarity is not marketing — it is the reason the name has value at all.

A domain is priced by the market. A category is priced by what it lets its owner become.
