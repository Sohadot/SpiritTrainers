## Not a domain. A category position.

A domain sale asks: *"What is this URL worth to you today?"*

A category-position acquisition asks: *"What is it worth to hold the name of a
market before it consolidates — and what does it cost you if someone else holds
it first?"*

The same asset, valued two ways. This one is framed as the second.

## What the acquisition includes

Acquiring the position transfers a coherent bundle, not a bare name:

1. **The exact-match domain** — SpiritTrainers.com, the clean top-level identity.
2. **The category thesis** — a public, reasoned case for the name; the buyer
   inherits the argument already made.
3. **The fixed definition** — a stable internal meaning that protects the name
   from drift.
4. **The market architecture** — the map of every market the name can be
   activated into.
5. **The diligence record** — prior uses, semantic collisions, evidence, and the
   claim's boundaries, disclosed rather than hidden.
6. **The activation optionality** — brand, institute, methodology, division, or
   defensive hold, subject to the buyer's own legal and trademark clearance.

A domain sale transfers only the first. This transfers all six.

## The cost of inaction

> The name is either an asset on your balance sheet or a headline on a
> competitor's. There is no third state where it stays neutral forever.

Clean category names do not stay open. Securing one is less an opportunity to
seize than a position to hold before it is taken.

## What is not claimed

To keep the case honest and therefore credible, the asset does not promise
adoption, revenue, or traffic; does not claim the words have never been used or
that the category is already established; does not claim that owning the domain
confers ownership of the phrase; and does not guarantee the buyer's outcome. It
claims something narrower: the market has not consolidated around a name for this
work, and this is the exact-match name and the case for it. It transfers a
*position*, not a result. Activating it — and clearing it for the buyer's own use
— is the buyer's work.
