## Not a domain. A category.

A domain sale asks: *"What is this URL worth to you today?"*

A category acquisition asks: *"What is it worth to own the name of a market
before it consolidates — and what does it cost you if someone else owns it
first?"*

The same asset, valued two ways. This one is framed as the second.

## What the acquisition includes

Acquiring the category transfers a coherent bundle, not a bare name:

1. **The canonical name** — SpiritTrainers.com, the clean top-level identity.
2. **The thesis** — a public, reasoned case that the name *is* a category; the
   buyer inherits the argument already made.
3. **The meaning** — a fixed, defensible definition that protects the name from
   drift.
4. **The positioning** — the map of every market the name can enter.
5. **The optionality** — the right to activate the name as a brand, institute,
   certification, division, or defensive hold.

A domain sale transfers only the first. This transfers all five.

## The cost of inaction

> The name is either an asset on your balance sheet or a headline on a
> competitor's. There is no third state where it stays neutral forever.

Clean category names do not stay available. Ownership is less an opportunity to
seize than a position to secure before it is taken.

## What is not claimed

To keep the case honest and therefore credible, the asset does not promise
revenue, traffic, or growth; does not claim the category is already established;
and does not guarantee the buyer's outcome. It transfers a *position*. Activating
it is the buyer's work.
