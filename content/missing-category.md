## The layer beneath the others

Most human-development markets operate on the top three layers of a person —
skill, knowledge, intelligence. Each has a mature market and a category name. The
layer beneath them, the one that governs the other three under pressure, has
practitioners everywhere and no top-level name.

```
SKILL        — what you can do
KNOWLEDGE    — what you know
INTELLIGENCE — how fast you process
─────────────────────────────────
SPIRIT       — how you use all of it   ← the unnamed layer
```

## Already a category, without a name

We already treat "X Trainers" as categories: athletic, personal, corporate, dog,
AI. Each began as a mere description of an activity, then the market started
treating the phrase as a *thing* — with its own professionals and standards.

*Spirit Trainers* is the entry the list is missing. The phrase reads as if the
category already exists — and the small surprise you feel that it doesn't is the
opportunity.

## Why the gap exists

The interior was never *unimportant*; it was *distributed*. Religion, sport,
therapy, philosophy, the military, education, and leadership each owned a
fragment and named it in their own dialect. No single field had the incentive —
or the neutrality — to name the whole.

A name that no discipline owns is exactly the kind of name that can sit *above*
all of them.

## One name, many markets

Because the interior it names is the same interior every serious institution is
already developing, one name can be worn across many markets:

- Executive & leadership coaching
- Corporate wellbeing & resilience
- Military & first-responder readiness
- Education & youth development
- Sports psychology & mental performance
- Rehabilitation & recovery
- Higher-education institutes

The point is not that the category is large. It is that the category is
**singular across many industries**: one name, many markets. That is what makes
the name valuable to all of them at once.
