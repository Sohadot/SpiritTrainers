The Journal

Essays on names, categories, and positioning.

Part of the thesis, not a side blog. Each essay develops one facet of a single idea: how a word becomes the identity of a category.

Essay 01

Why Categories Need Names

A name is an act of gathering: it turns a scatter of instances into a category people can join, study, and defend.

 Essay 02

When a Name Becomes Infrastructure

An entire market can run on a single phrase. Like good infrastructure, the word disappears from view precisely because everything depends on it.

 Essay 03

The Difference Between a Profession and a Category

A profession is a role; a category is the space roles sit in. Confusing the two is the most common way people misjudge what a name is worth.

 Essay 04

Why Generic Brands Rarely Lead Categories

The most descriptive name rarely wins. Category-leading names are not descriptions — they are compressed arguments about the space.

 Essay 05

The Linguistics of Human Development

Grit, resilience, readiness, character — a domain rich in specific words and empty of the one general word that would gather them.

 Essay 06

The Name That Arrives Before Its Market

Some names arrive early enough to help decide what a category becomes — framing the space while its shape is still soft.

 Essay 07

Why the Best Names Feel Inevitable

The strongest category names are not experienced as invented but recognized — as if the word had been missing rather than absent.

 Essay 08

The Defensibility of a Category Name

A product can be out-built; the word a market thinks in cannot. Owning the category name is a position competitors must route around, not through.
